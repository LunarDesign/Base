Which heart sound marks the end of diastole and the beginning of systole? a) S3 b) S4 c) S1 d) S2 Correct Answer: c) S1 Explanation: S1, also known as the "lub" sound, is caused by the closure of the AV valves (mitral and tricuspid). It is loudest at the apex of the heart and signifies the transition from diastole to systole. 2. What is the anatomical location where S2 is loudest? a) 4th ICS, left sternal border b) 5th ICS, midclavicular line c) 3rd ICS, midclavicular line d) 2nd ICS, right sternal border Correct Answer: d) 2nd ICS, right sternal border Explanation: S2, or the "dub" sound, is caused by the closure of the semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonic). It is loudest at the base of the heart, which is anatomically located at the 2nd intercostal space (ICS) at the right sternal border. 3. Which of the following conditions is NOT associated with the presence of an S3 heart sound? a) Mitral insufficiency b) Heart failure c) Myocardial infarction d) Pulmonary hypertension Correct Answer: c) Myocardial infarction Explanation: S3 is caused by a rapid rush of blood into a dilated ventricle and is commonly associated with heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, and mitral/aortic/tricuspid insufficiency. Myocardial infarction is more commonly associated with an S4 sound. 4. What is a key characteristic of an S4 heart sound? a) Associated with heart failure b) Loudest at the base of the heart c) Occurs early in diastole, right after S2 d) Caused by atrial contraction into a noncompliant ventricle Correct Answer: d) Caused by atrial contraction into a noncompliant ventricle Explanation: S4 occurs due to atrial contraction forcing blood into a noncompliant ventricle. It is best heard at the apex with the bell of the stethoscope and is associated with myocardial ischemia, ventricular hypertrophy, and aortic stenosis. 5. Why is S4 not heard in the presence of atrial fibrillation? a) Blood flow is too rapid into the ventricles. b) There is no atrial contraction. c) The ventricles are noncompliant. d) It is masked by S1 and S2 sounds. Correct Answer: b) There is no atrial contraction. Explanation: S4 is generated by atrial contraction. In atrial fibrillation, there is a loss of organized atrial contraction, making the S4 sound absent. 6. Which heart sound is associated with a "Kentucky" rhythm? a) S1 b) S3 c) S4 d) S2 Correct Answer: b) S3 Explanation: S3, also called a ventricular gallop, has a "Kentucky" rhythm and occurs early in diastole following S2. It is often linked to heart failure and rapid ventricular filling. 7. Which heart sound is commonly linked to myocardial ischemia? a) S3 b) S1 c) S2 d) S4 Correct Answer: d) S4 Explanation: S4, or atrial gallop, occurs when blood is pushed into a stiff or noncompliant ventricle. It is commonly associated with myocardial ischemia, infarction, hypertension, and ventricular hypertrophy. 8. What is a pericardial friction rub associated with? a) Atrial septal defect b) Ventricular septal defect c) Pericarditis d) Aortic stenosis Correct Answer: c) Pericarditis Explanation: A pericardial friction rub is due to inflammation of the pericardium (pericarditis). It is often associated with pain on deep inspiration and may vary with position changes. 9. What condition is characterized by a wide, fixed splitting of S2? a) Aortic stenosis b) Right bundle branch block (RBBB) c) Pulmonary embolism d) Mitral valve stenosis Correct Answer: b) Right bundle branch block (RBBB) Explanation: A wide, fixed splitting of S2 occurs in right bundle branch block (RBBB) due to delayed closure of the pulmonic valve. 10. Which auscultatory point corresponds to the apex of the heart? a) 4th ICS, left sternal border b) 2nd ICS, right sternal border c) 5th ICS, midclavicular line d) 3rd ICS, right sternal border Correct Answer: c) 5th ICS, midclavicular line Explanation: The apex of the heart is the mitral area, where S1 is loudest. It is anatomically located at the 5th intercostal space (ICS), midclavicular line. 11. What does a pulse pressure of 40–60 mmHg typically indicate? a) Decreased systemic vascular resistance (SVR) b) Normal pulse pressure c) Narrowing of pulse pressure d) Widening of pulse pressure Correct Answer: b) Normal pulse pressure Explanation: Pulse pressure is calculated as systolic blood pressure minus diastolic blood pressure. A normal pulse pressure is 40–60 mmHg, as seen in a blood pressure of 120/80 mmHg. 12. What does a decrease in systolic pressure with little change in diastolic pressure typically indicate? a) Increase in systemic vascular resistance b) Drop in coronary perfusion c) Widening of pulse pressure due to vasodilation d) Narrowing of pulse pressure due to hypovolemia Correct Answer: d) Narrowing of pulse pressure due to hypovolemia Explanation: Narrowing of pulse pressure occurs when systolic pressure decreases while diastolic pressure remains constant or increases. This is commonly seen in severe hypovolemia or a significant drop in cardiac output. 13. Which of the following conditions is associated with a widened pulse pressure? a) Acute myocardial infarction b) Severe hypovolemia c) Dilated cardiomyopathy d) Septic shock Correct Answer: d) Septic shock Explanation: A widened pulse pressure, such as 100/38 mmHg, indicates vasodilation and a drop in systemic vascular resistance, which are often seen in sepsis or septic shock. 14. During which phase of the cardiac cycle are coronary arteries perfused? a) Early diastole b) Throughout diastole c) Late systole d) Early systole Correct Answer: b) Throughout diastole Explanation: Coronary arteries are perfused during diastole because this phase allows for reduced myocardial tension and adequate blood flow into the coronary circulation. 15. Why does cardiac output drop during extreme tachyarrhythmias? a) Narrowing of pulse pressure b) Excessive stroke volume c) Decreased diastolic filling time d) Increased systemic vascular resistance Correct Answer: c) Decreased diastolic filling time Explanation: Extreme tachyarrhythmias reduce diastolic filling time, leading to decreased ventricular filling and, consequently, reduced cardiac output and blood pressure. 16. What is the primary cause of valve closure in normal heart sounds? a) Increased coronary perfusion b) Pressure changes across the valve c) Decreased systemic vascular resistance d) Myocardial contraction Correct Answer: b) Pressure changes across the valve Explanation: Normal heart sounds, S1 and S2, are caused by valve closure. The valves open and close due to pressure changes in the chambers above and below the valve. 17. What type of murmur occurs during systole in a patient with aortic stenosis? a) Continuous murmur b) Systolic murmur c) Holosystolic murmur d) Diastolic murmur Correct Answer: b) Systolic murmur Explanation: Aortic stenosis causes a systolic murmur because the valve is narrowed, creating turbulence during the high-pressure ejection phase of systole. 18. Which of the following is NOT a cause of valvular heart disease? a) Coronary artery disease b) Rheumatic fever c) Acute myocardial infarction d) Widened pulse pressure Correct Answer: d) Widened pulse pressure Explanation: Widened pulse pressure is not a direct cause of valvular heart disease. Valvular heart disease can result from conditions like rheumatic fever, CAD, and acute MI, among others. 19. What is the key difference between systole and diastole? a) Diastole involves filling, while systole involves ejection. b) Diastole is associated with coronary artery perfusion, while systole is not. c) Systole is one-third longer than diastole. d) Systole involves filling, while diastole involves ejection. Correct Answer: a) Diastole involves filling, while systole involves ejection. Explanation: Systole is the high-pressure ejection phase when blood is pumped out of the heart, while diastole is the low-pressure filling phase. 20. Which genetic condition is a known cause of valvular heart disease? a) Bicuspid aortic valve b) Dilated cardiomyopathy c) Myocardial ischemia d) Acute rheumatic fever Correct Answer: a) Bicuspid aortic valve Explanation: A bicuspid aortic valve is a genetic condition that predisposes individuals to valvular heart disease, including stenosis or insufficiency. 21. What is the characteristic feature of murmurs caused by valve insufficiency? a) Occur when the valve is open b) Are always acute c) Occur when the valve is closed d) Develop only in chronic conditions Correct Answer: c) Occur when the valve is closed Explanation: Murmurs of insufficiency (regurgitation) occur when the valve is closed because blood flows backward through the valve, which should be tightly sealed. 22. Which of the following murmurs is most likely to occur during systole? a) Mitral stenosis b) Tricuspid stenosis c) Aortic insufficiency d) Mitral insufficiency Correct Answer: d) Mitral insufficiency Explanation: During systole, the AV valves (mitral and tricuspid) are closed, and mitral insufficiency results in a systolic murmur due to backward blood flow through the mitral valve. 23. What heart condition is associated with giant V-waves on a pulmonary artery occlusion pressure tracing? a) Tricuspid stenosis b) Mitral insufficiency c) Aortic stenosis d) Pulmonic insufficiency Correct Answer: b) Mitral insufficiency Explanation: Mitral insufficiency causes backward flow of blood into the left atrium during systole, leading to large, giant V-waves on the pulmonary artery occlusion pressure tracing. 24. Which murmur is most commonly associated with an acute myocardial infarction? a) Aortic stenosis b) Ventricular septal defect c) Mitral stenosis d) Tricuspid insufficiency Correct Answer: b) Ventricular septal defect Explanation: Ventricular septal defect (VSD) can occur due to septal rupture following an acute myocardial infarction. It results in a systolic murmur heard at the left sternal border, 5th intercostal space. 25. Which diastolic murmur is often linked with atrial fibrillation due to atrial enlargement? a) Pulmonic stenosis b) Tricuspid insufficiency c) Mitral stenosis d) Aortic stenosis Correct Answer: c) Mitral stenosis Explanation: Mitral stenosis causes atrial enlargement over time, which predisposes the patient to atrial fibrillation. 26. During which phase of the cardiac cycle are semilunar valves open? a) Early diastole b) Systole c) Late diastole d) Both systole and diastole Correct Answer: b) Systole Explanation: Semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonic) are open during systole to allow ejection of blood from the ventricles into the arteries. 27. Which murmur is caused by aortic insufficiency? a) Continuous murmur b) Systolic murmur c) Diastolic murmur d) Mid-systolic murmur Correct Answer: c) Diastolic murmur Explanation: Aortic insufficiency occurs when the aortic valve does not close properly during diastole, leading to regurgitation of blood into the left ventricle. 28. What is a common characteristic of murmurs caused by stenosis? a) They occur when the valve is closed. b) They develop acutely. c) They occur when the valve is open. d) They only occur with AV valves. Correct Answer: c) They occur when the valve is open. Explanation: Murmurs caused by stenosis occur when the valve is open because the narrowing restricts blood flow during its normal phase of function. 29. Which condition is most likely associated with tricuspid stenosis? a) Pulmonary artery occlusion b) Systolic murmur c) Right atrial enlargement d) Giant V-waves Correct Answer: c) Right atrial enlargement Explanation: Tricuspid stenosis, a diastolic murmur, leads to restricted blood flow from the right atrium to the right ventricle, causing right atrial enlargement over time. 30. What type of murmur is heard in pulmonic stenosis? a) Continuous murmur b) Systolic murmur c) Diastolic murmur d) Holosystolic murmur Correct Answer: b) Systolic murmur Explanation: Pulmonic stenosis creates a systolic murmur because the narrowed pulmonic valve restricts blood flow from the right ventricle into the pulmonary artery during systole. 31. When does a murmur caused by mitral insufficiency occur? a) During atrial contraction b) During diastole c) During isovolumetric relaxation d) During systole Correct Answer: d) During systole Explanation: Mitral insufficiency occurs when the mitral valve is closed, which happens during systole as the left ventricle contracts to eject blood into the aorta. 32. A murmur caused by mitral stenosis occurs during which phase of the cardiac cycle? a) Systole b) Ventricular ejection c) Diastole d) Isovolumetric contraction Correct Answer: c) Diastole Explanation: Mitral stenosis occurs when the mitral valve is open during diastole, allowing blood to flow from the left atrium to the left ventricle. 33. During which phase of the cardiac cycle is a murmur caused by aortic insufficiency heard? a) Diastole b) Isovolumetric contraction c) Late systole d) Systole Correct Answer: a) Diastole Explanation: Aortic insufficiency occurs when the aortic valve is closed, which happens during diastole. Blood flows back into the left ventricle, causing the murmur. 34. A murmur due to aortic stenosis is most likely to occur during: a) Diastole b) Systole c) Isovolumetric relaxation d) Atrial systole Correct Answer: b) Systole Explanation: Aortic stenosis occurs when the aortic valve is open, which happens during systole as blood is ejected from the left ventricle into the aorta. 35. Murmurs caused by a ventricular septal defect (VSD) occur during: a) Diastole b) Systole c) Both systole and diastole d) Late diastole Correct Answer: b) Systole Explanation: VSD murmurs occur during systole, as the high pressure in the left ventricle forces blood through the defect into the lower-pressure right ventricle. 36. Which heart condition associated with an acute myocardial infarction results in a systolic murmur heard at the apex? a) Mitral stenosis b) Ventricular septal defect c) Papillary muscle dysfunction d) Aortic stenosis Correct Answer: c) Papillary muscle dysfunction Explanation: Papillary muscle dysfunction affects the mitral valve's ability to close properly, leading to acute mitral regurgitation, which causes a systolic murmur loudest at the apex. 37. A loud systolic murmur at the apex that requires emergency surgery is most likely caused by: a) Mitral stenosis b) Aortic regurgitation c) Papillary muscle rupture d) Tricuspid stenosis Correct Answer: c) Papillary muscle rupture Explanation: Papillary muscle rupture leads to severe mitral regurgitation, which is a surgical emergency. The murmur is loudest at the apex and may reach Grade V or VI. 38. A murmur due to papillary muscle dysfunction after myocardial infarction is typically: a) Continuous and heard over the sternum b) Systolic and heard at the apex c) Diastolic and heard at the base d) Diastolic and heard at the apex Correct Answer: b) Systolic and heard at the apex Explanation: Papillary muscle dysfunction after myocardial infarction causes acute mitral regurgitation, resulting in a systolic murmur loudest at the apex. 39. What is the auscultation point for a murmur caused by a ventricular septal defect (VSD)? a) Left sternal border, 5th intercostal space b) Right sternal border, 2nd intercostal space c) Apex of the heart d) Left sternal border, 2nd intercostal space Correct Answer: a) Left sternal border, 5th intercostal space Explanation: A VSD murmur is heard at the left sternal border, 5th intercostal space, due to blood flow from the left ventricle to the right ventricle during systole. 40. What is the primary structural cause of a murmur due to mitral regurgitation in myocardial infarction? a) Tricuspid valve stenosis b) Papillary muscle dysfunction or rupture c) Pulmonic valve insufficiency d) Ventricular wall aneurysm Correct Answer: b) Papillary muscle dysfunction or rupture Explanation: Mitral regurgitation in myocardial infarction is often caused by papillary muscle dysfunction or rupture, which prevents the mitral valve from closing properly. 41. Which of the following is a characteristic of unstable angina? a) Troponin negative, chest pain at rest, ST depression or T-wave inversion b) Troponin positive, unrelenting chest pain, ST depression c) Transient ST elevation, relieved by nitroglycerin d) Troponin positive, ST elevation in contiguous leads Correct Answer: a) Troponin negative, chest pain at rest, ST depression or T-wave inversion Explanation: Unstable angina is characterized by chest pain at rest, unpredictability, being troponin negative, and ECG changes such as ST depression or T-wave inversion. 42. What is a defining feature of a STEMI compared to NSTEMI? a) ST depression and T-wave inversion on ECG b) Transient ST elevation caused by coronary spasm c) Troponin negative and relief with nitroglycerin d) Troponin positive with ST elevation in at least two contiguous leads Correct Answer: d) Troponin positive with ST elevation in at least two contiguous leads Explanation: STEMI is defined by elevated troponin levels and ST elevation in at least two contiguous ECG leads, indicating a complete blockage of a coronary artery. 43. Which type of acute coronary syndrome is associated with coronary artery spasm? a) Stable angina b) Variant (Prinzmetal’s) angina c) STEMI d) NSTEMI Correct Answer: b) Variant (Prinzmetal’s) angina Explanation: Variant or Prinzmetal’s angina is caused by coronary artery spasm, which may or may not be associated with atherosclerotic lesions. It is often transient and relieved with nitroglycerin. 44. In which population are atypical symptoms of an MI most commonly observed? a) Patients with a history of stable angina b) Young athletes c) Women, individuals with diabetes, and the elderly d) Middle-aged men Correct Answer: c) Women, individuals with diabetes, and the elderly Explanation: Women, individuals with diabetes, and the elderly often present with atypical symptoms of MI, such as nausea, fatigue, syncope, or acute delirium, rather than classic chest pain. 45. Which of the following ECG findings is associated with unstable angina? a) ST elevation in contiguous leads b) ST depression or T-wave inversion c) No significant ECG changes d) Transient ST elevation with relief by nitroglycerin Correct Answer: b) ST depression or T-wave inversion Explanation: Unstable angina typically shows ST depression or T-wave inversion on the ECG, reflecting ischemia without myocardial infarction. 46. Which of the following is characteristic of NSTEMI? a) Troponin negative, transient ST elevation b) Troponin positive, ST depression, or T-wave inversion c) Troponin negative, ST depression, and chest pain relieved by rest d) Troponin positive, ST elevation in contiguous leads Correct Answer: b) Troponin positive, ST depression, or T-wave inversion Explanation: NSTEMI is defined by elevated troponin levels and ECG changes such as ST depression or T-wave inversion. It indicates a partial blockage of a coronary artery. 47. Which condition is most likely to respond to nitroglycerin with relief of symptoms and normalization of ST segments? a) NSTEMI b) STEMI c) Stable angina d) Variant (Prinzmetal’s) angina Correct Answer: d) Variant (Prinzmetal’s) angina Explanation: Variant angina, caused by coronary artery spasm, often resolves with nitroglycerin, which relaxes the smooth muscles of the coronary arteries. 48. What is the most common ECG finding in a patient with a STEMI? a) ST depression b) Peaked T-waves c) ST elevation in two or more contiguous leads d) T-wave inversion Correct Answer: c) ST elevation in two or more contiguous leads Explanation: STEMI is characterized by ST elevation in at least two contiguous leads on the ECG, indicating significant myocardial injury. 49. Which type of angina is cyclic, occurs at rest, and is associated with transient ST segment elevation? a) Unstable angina b) Silent ischemia c) Variant (Prinzmetal’s) angina d) Stable angina Correct Answer: c) Variant (Prinzmetal’s) angina Explanation: Variant angina is a type of unstable angina that occurs at rest, is cyclic, and is associated with transient ST segment elevation due to coronary artery spasm. 50. What is the typical ECG finding in stable angina? a) ST elevation with relief by nitroglycerin b) ST elevation in contiguous leads c) Normal ECG at rest, ST depression during activity d) T-wave inversion at rest Correct Answer: c) Normal ECG at rest, ST depression during activity Explanation: Stable angina typically shows a normal ECG at rest, but during physical activity, ST depression may occur due to transient myocardial ischemia. 51. Which of the following medications is recommended to be given immediately and chewed for patients with acute chest pain? a) Nitroglycerin b) Clopidogrel c) Morphine d) Aspirin Correct Answer: d) Aspirin Explanation: Aspirin should be administered immediately and chewed in cases of acute chest pain to improve morbidity and mortality by inhibiting platelet aggregation. 52. What is the primary purpose of obtaining an ECG within 10 minutes of a patient presenting with acute chest pain? a) To determine if anticoagulants are necessary b) To assess electrolyte imbalances c) To measure cardiac biomarkers d) To categorize the condition as STEMI or NSTEMI/unstable angina Correct Answer: d) To categorize the condition as STEMI or NSTEMI/unstable angina Explanation: A rapid ECG allows the classification of chest pain into STEMI or NSTEMI/unstable angina, guiding immediate management and risk stratification. 53. Which anticoagulant is commonly used in the management of acute coronary syndrome (ACS)? a) Heparin or enoxaparin b) Warfarin c) Rivaroxaban d) Dabigatran Correct Answer: a) Heparin or enoxaparin Explanation: Heparin or enoxaparin is used to reduce the formation of thrombi in acute coronary syndrome, preventing progression of ischemia. 54. What is a contraindication for the use of beta blockers in acute coronary syndrome? a) Normal blood pressure and heart rate b) Presence of STEMI in the inferior leads c) History of stable angina d) Concurrent use of phosphodiesterase inhibitors like sildenafil Correct Answer: d) Concurrent use of phosphodiesterase inhibitors like sildenafil Explanation: Beta blockers are contraindicated in patients taking phosphodiesterase inhibitors such as sildenafil due to the risk of severe hypotension. 55. Which ECG leads are associated with an anterior wall myocardial infarction? a) V1, V2, V3, V4 b) V3R, V4R c) I, aVL, V5, V6 d) II, III, aVF Correct Answer: a) V1, V2, V3, V4 Explanation: Changes in leads V1–V4 are indicative of an anterior wall myocardial infarction, which involves the left anterior descending artery. 56. Which medication should be avoided in ACS if the condition is due to cocaine use? a) Non-cardioselective beta blockers like propranolol b) Morphine c) Aspirin d) Cardioselective beta blockers like metoprolol Correct Answer: a) Non-cardioselective beta blockers like propranolol Explanation: Non-cardioselective beta blockers such as propranolol should be avoided in cocaine-induced ACS because they can exacerbate coronary vasospasm. 57. What does ST-segment elevation in leads II, III, and aVF indicate? a) Left anterior descending artery involvement b) Circumflex artery involvement, lateral LV infarction c) Right coronary artery involvement, posterior LV infarction d) Right coronary artery involvement, inferior LV infarction Correct Answer: d) Right coronary artery involvement, inferior LV infarction Explanation: ST-segment elevation in leads II, III, and aVF indicates an inferior myocardial infarction due to right coronary artery involvement. 58. Which of the following is an antiplatelet agent used in ACS management? a) Enoxaparin b) Morphine c) Metoprolol d) Clopidogrel Correct Answer: d) Clopidogrel Explanation: Clopidogrel is an antiplatelet agent used in ACS to prevent platelet aggregation and reduce the risk of thrombotic events. 59. What lab tests are important in the initial evaluation of acute chest pain? a) PT, PTT, cardiac biomarkers, lipid profile, and renal function tests b) Complete blood count, glucose tolerance test, and blood culture c) Liver function tests, urine analysis, and arterial blood gas d) Magnesium levels, coagulation profile, and thyroid function tests Correct Answer: a) PT, PTT, cardiac biomarkers, lipid profile, and renal function tests Explanation: PT, PTT, cardiac biomarkers, lipid profile, and renal function tests help assess myocardial damage, coagulation status, and overall risk in acute chest pain. 60. What ECG changes are associated with a lateral wall myocardial infarction? a) ST elevation in II, III, aVF b) ST depression in V1, V2 c) ST elevation in I, aVL, V5, V6 d) ST elevation in V3R, V4R Correct Answer: c) ST elevation in I, aVL, V5, V6 Explanation: ST elevation in leads I, aVL, V5, and V6 is indicative of a lateral wall myocardial infarction due to circumflex artery involvement. 61. Which coronary artery is most commonly associated with an inferior myocardial infarction (MI)? a) Left circumflex artery b) Posterior descending artery c) Left anterior descending ( LAD ) artery d) Right coronary artery ( RCA ) Correct Answer: d) Right coronary artery ( RCA ) Explanation: Inferior MI is commonly caused by occlusion of the RCA, which supplies the inferior wall of the left ventricle. 62. What ECG changes are typically seen in an inferior wall MI? a) ST elevation in I and aVL b) ST elevation in V1–V4 c) ST elevation in V4R and V5 d) ST elevation in II, III, and aVF Correct Answer: d) ST elevation in II, III, and aVF Explanation: ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF is diagnostic of an inferior wall MI. 63. Which conduction disturbances are associated with inferior wall MI? a) Right bundle branch block and ventricular tachycardia b) 2nd-degree Type II AV block and right bundle branch block c) 2nd-degree Type I AV block and sinus bradycardia d) Complete heart block and atrial fibrillation Correct Answer: c) 2nd-degree Type I AV block and sinus bradycardia Explanation: Inferior MI is associated with AV conduction disturbances such as 2nd-degree Type I AV block, sinus bradycardia, and sometimes complete heart block due to RCA involvement. 64. What treatment should be avoided in a patient with a right ventricular (RV) infarction? a) Positive inotropes b) Antiplatelet agents c) Preload reducers like nitrates and diuretics d) Fluids Correct Answer: c) Preload reducers like nitrates and diuretics Explanation: Nitrates and diuretics reduce preload, which can worsen hypotension in patients with an RV infarction. 65. What signs and symptoms are suggestive of a right ventricular infarction? a) Bradycardia and ST elevation in V1–V4 b) Jugular venous distension, hypotension, clear lungs c) Severe chest pain radiating to the jaw d) Pulmonary edema and tachycardia Correct Answer: b) Jugular venous distension, hypotension, clear lungs Explanation: Right ventricular infarction typically presents with jugular venous distension, hypotension, clear lung fields, and bradyarrhythmias. 66. Which coronary artery is typically occluded in an anterior MI? a) Posterior descending artery b) Left anterior descending (LAD) artery c) Right coronary artery d) Left circumflex artery Correct Answer: b) Left anterior descending (LAD) artery Explanation: Anterior MI is associated with occlusion of the LAD artery, which supplies the anterior wall of the left ventricle. 67. Which ECG leads show ST elevation in an anterior wall MI? a) I, aVL, V5, V6 b) V1–V4 c) II, III, aVF d) V4R, V5R Correct Answer: b) V1–V4 Explanation: ST elevation in leads V1–V4 indicates an anterior wall MI due to LAD artery occlusion. 68. What ominous conduction disturbance may occur in anterior MI? a) Sinus bradycardia b) 2nd-degree Type II AV block or right bundle branch block (RBBB) c) Atrial fibrillation d) Complete heart block Correct Answer: b) 2nd-degree Type II AV block or right bundle branch block (RBBB) Explanation: Anterior MI may cause 2nd-degree Type II AV block or RBBB, as the LAD artery supplies the bundle of His. These findings indicate a poor prognosis. 69. Which ECG leads are associated with a lateral wall MI? a) II, III, aVF b) V4R, V5R c) V1–V4 d) I, aVL, V5, V6 Correct Answer: d) I, aVL, V5, V6 Explanation: Lateral wall MI is associated with ST elevation in leads I, aVL (high lateral) and V5, V6 (low lateral), typically due to circumflex artery involvement. 70. What systolic murmur may develop in a patient with an inferior MI? a) Ventricular septal defect murmur b) Tricuspid stenosis murmur c) Aortic stenosis murmur d) Mitral valve regurgitation murmur due to papillary muscle rupture Correct Answer: d) Mitral valve regurgitation murmur due to papillary muscle rupture Explanation: Inferior MI can lead to papillary muscle rupture, resulting in mitral valve regurgitation and a systolic murmur. 71. What is the time goal for door-to-balloon inflation in STEMI patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI)? a) 120 minutes b) 90 minutes c) 60 minutes d) 30 minutes Correct Answer: b) 90 minutes Explanation: The standard guideline for STEMI treatment is to achieve door-to-balloon inflation within 90 minutes to restore coronary blood flow and reduce myocardial damage. 72. What is a key eligibility criterion for fibrinolytic therapy in STEMI? a) Chest pain responsive to sublingual nitroglycerin b) Chest pain lasting 30 minutes unresponsive to nitroglycerin c) Onset of chest pain > 12 hours d) ST elevation in one ECG lead Correct Answer: b) Chest pain lasting 30 minutes unresponsive to nitroglycerin Explanation: Fibrinolytic therapy is indicated for patients with chest pain lasting at least 30 minutes, unresponsive to nitroglycerin, and occurring within 12 hours of symptom onset with ECG findings like ST elevation. 73. Which sign may indicate reocclusion following PCI for STEMI? a) Resolution of ST elevation and chest pain b) Persistent ST elevation with recurrent chest pain c) Decreasing blood pressure without chest pain d) Sinus tachycardia and hypotension Correct Answer: b) Persistent ST elevation with recurrent chest pain Explanation: Persistent or recurrent ST elevation with recurrent chest pain after PCI suggests coronary artery reocclusion, requiring immediate medical attention. 74. What is the recommended action for vasovagal reactions during sheath removal post-PCI? a) Use fibrinolytic drugs b) Increase oxygen therapy c) Administer fluids and atropine d) Perform immediate sheath reinsertion Correct Answer: c) Administer fluids and atropine Explanation: Vasovagal reactions, characterized by hypotension, bradycardia, and pallor during sheath removal, are managed with fluids and atropine to stabilize the patient. 75. Which symptom is most indicative of retroperitoneal bleeding in a post-PCI patient? a) Increasing chest pain and ST elevation b) Persistent sinus tachycardia c) Severe low back pain and sudden hypotension d) Swelling at the sheath site Correct Answer: c) Severe low back pain and sudden hypotension Explanation: Retroperitoneal bleeding may present with severe low back pain, sudden hypotension, and symptoms of shock, requiring urgent intervention. 76. Which condition is an absolute contraindication to fibrinolytic therapy? a) Recent minor surgery b) History of ischemic stroke within 3 months c) Suspected aortic dissection d) Menstrual bleeding Correct Answer: c) Suspected aortic dissection Explanation: Suspected aortic dissection is a critical contraindication for fibrinolytic therapy, as it increases the risk of catastrophic bleeding. 77. During manual pressure application for sheath site bleeding, how long should pressure be applied if the patient is on GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors? a) 15 minutes b) 10 minutes c) 30 minutes d) 20 minutes Correct Answer: c) 30 minutes Explanation: Manual pressure should be applied for a minimum of 30 minutes if the patient is receiving GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors to achieve effective hemostasis. 78. What ECG finding is a criterion for initiating fibrinolytic therapy in STEMI? a) T-wave inversion in inferior leads b) New onset right bundle branch block c) ST depression in precordial leads d) ST elevation in 2 or more contiguous leads Correct Answer: d) ST elevation in 2 or more contiguous leads Explanation: ST elevation in 2 or more contiguous leads or new onset left bundle branch block (LBBB) are key ECG findings that qualify a patient for fibrinolytic therapy. 79. Which symptom is most likely to occur during a vasovagal reaction in a patient post-PCI? a) Pallor, bradycardia, and nausea b) Pulmonary edema and hypertension c) Chest pain and cyanosis d) Tachycardia and restlessness Correct Answer: a) Pallor, bradycardia, and nausea Explanation: Vasovagal reactions are characterized by bradycardia, pallor, nausea, and hypotension without compensatory tachycardia. 80. Why are nitrates contraindicated in patients with a right ventricular infarction? a) They exacerbate myocardial ischemia. b) They increase the risk of arrhythmias. c) They reduce myocardial oxygen demand. d) They decrease preload, worsening hypotension. Which of the following indicates successful reperfusion in a patient treated for STEMI? a) Absence of reperfusion arrhythmias b) Decline in troponin levels and persistent VT c) Persistent chest pain and bradycardia d) Resolution of ST segment deviations and chest pain relief Correct Answer: d) Resolution of ST segment deviations and chest pain relief Explanation: Successful reperfusion is evidenced by chest pain relief, resolution of ST segment deviations, and reperfusion arrhythmias such as VT, VF, or AIVR, indicating restored blood flow. 82.
