Which of the following mediates platelet activation during the platelet phase of clot formation? a) ADP secretion b) Factor X c) Vitamin K d) Thrombin Correct Answer: a) ADP secretion Explanation: ADP secretion is a key step in platelet activation. When platelets are activated, they release ADP, which enhances further platelet recruitment and activation at the site of injury. 2. In the intrinsic coagulation pathway, what triggers the initiation of clotting? a) Tissue thromboplastin release b) Extensive myocardial infarction c) Endothelial vascular injury d) Malignancies Correct Answer: c) Endothelial vascular injury Explanation: The intrinsic pathway is triggered by vascular endothelial injury, which exposes subendothelial collagen and activates clotting factors. 3. Which pathway is activated in the case of extensive trauma? a) COX pathway b) Extrinsic pathway c) Intrinsic pathway d) Platelet pathway Correct Answer: b) Extrinsic pathway Explanation: The extrinsic pathway is stimulated by tissue injury, which leads to the release of tissue thromboplastin, a key event in trauma-induced clotting. 4. What is the role of thromboxane A2 in platelet plug formation? a) Initiates fibrin clot formation b) Stimulates the COX pathway c) Enhances ADP secretion d) Mediates platelet aggregation Correct Answer: d) Mediates platelet aggregation Explanation: Thromboxane A2 is produced during platelet activation and serves to further aggregate platelets at the site of injury, promoting platelet plug formation. 5. What clinical situation activates the intrinsic coagulation pathway? a) Dissecting aortic aneurysm b) Malignancies c) Cell trauma from IABP (Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump) d) OB emergencies Correct Answer: c) Cell trauma from IABP (Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump) Explanation: The intrinsic pathway is triggered by vascular or cellular trauma, such as cell trauma caused by medical devices like an IABP. 6. Which medication inhibits thrombin formation in the coagulation pathway? a) Propylthiouracil b) Aspirin c) Heparin d) Coumadin Correct Answer: c) Heparin Explanation: Heparin inhibits thrombin activity by enhancing antithrombin III activity, preventing fibrin formation in the coagulation cascade. 7. What lab test measures the effectiveness of platelets in the clotting process? a) Platelet count b) Bleeding time c) PT/INR d) aPTT Correct Answer: b) Bleeding time Explanation: Bleeding time measures platelet function and assesses how well platelets contribute to clot formation at an injury site. 8. Which of the following is a characteristic of platelets during activation? a) Release of vitamin K-dependent factors b) Change in shape and appearance c) Production of tissue thromboplastin d) Formation of fibrin mesh Correct Answer: b) Change in shape and appearance Explanation: When platelets become activated in response to injury, they change shape and appearance, allowing them to adhere to the site of injury and form a platelet plug. 9. In a patient with ARDS, which pathway would most likely initiate clotting? a) Extrinsic pathway b) Intrinsic pathway c) Platelet pathway d) COX pathway Correct Answer: b) Intrinsic pathway Explanation: ARDS involves endothelial damage, which triggers the intrinsic pathway by exposing subendothelial components to circulating clotting factors. 10. What is the end product of the coagulation cascade that stabilizes a clot? a) Platelet plug b) Thromboxane A2 c) Fibrin mesh d) Fibrinogen Correct Answer: c) Fibrin mesh Explanation: The end product of the coagulation cascade is fibrin, which forms a stable fibrin mesh that reinforces the platelet plug to stop bleeding. 11. Which enzyme is primarily responsible for breaking down a blood clot? a) Plasmin b) Thrombin c) Protamine d) Vitamin K Correct Answer: a) Plasmin Explanation: Plasminogen is converted to plasmin, which breaks down fibrin and fibrinogen, leading to clot dissolution and the formation of fibrin split products. 12. What is the definitive lab test result for diagnosing Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)? a) Elevated D-dimer b) Increased PT/INR c) Elevated fibrin split products (FSP) d) Decreased fibrinogen Correct Answer: c) Elevated fibrin split products (FSP) Explanation: While D-dimer must be elevated, elevated fibrin split products (FSP) are considered the definitive lab test result for diagnosing DIC. 13. What reverses the anticoagulant effect of warfarin (Coumadin)? a) Vitamin K b) Protamine c) Aspirin d) Heparin Correct Answer: a) Vitamin K Explanation: Vitamin K is used to reverse the effects of warfarin by restoring the synthesis of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors. 14. Which of the following laboratory findings is associated with DIC? a) Normal D-dimer b) Decreased fibrin split products c) Decreased platelet count d) Increased fibrinogen levels Correct Answer: c) Decreased platelet count Explanation: DIC leads to a decreased platelet count due to consumption of clotting factors and platelets in microvascular thrombi. 15. What condition most commonly activates Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)? a) Endothelial damage b) Factor X deficiency c) Malnutrition d) Hypertension Correct Answer: a) Endothelial damage Explanation: Endothelial damage from conditions like sepsis, hypoxemia, ARDS, or shock is a common trigger for DIC, leading to activation of the fibrinolytic system. 16. Which anticoagulant can be reversed with protamine sulfate? a) Warfarin b) Coumadin c) Aspirin d) Heparin Correct Answer: d) Heparin Explanation: Protamine sulfate neutralizes heparin and reverses its anticoagulant effect, making it the treatment of choice for heparin overdose. 17. What happens to fibrinogen levels in Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)? a) They decrease b) They remain unchanged c) They increase d) They become unmeasurable Correct Answer: a) They decrease Explanation: In DIC, fibrinogen levels decrease due to the excessive consumption of clotting factors during widespread clot formation. 18. What is the normal range for fibrinogen levels in the blood? a) 50–100 mg/dL b) 10–50 mg/dL c) 400–600 mg/dL d) 200–400 mg/dL Correct Answer: d) 200–400 mg/dL Explanation: The normal range for fibrinogen levels is 200–400 mg/dL. Decreased levels are seen in conditions like DIC. 19. Which of the following is NOT a common cause of DIC? a) Freshwater drowning b) Sepsis c) Hypertension d) OB emergencies Correct Answer: c) Hypertension Explanation: Hypertension is not a typical cause of DIC. What is the first step in treating Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)? a) Administer fresh frozen plasma (FFP) b) Administer cryoprecipitate c) Identify and eliminate the underlying cause d) Start heparin therapy Correct Answer: c) Identify and eliminate the underlying cause Explanation: The first step in managing DIC is to identify and eliminate the underlying cause, as DIC is always secondary to another condition. 22. Which of the following blood components is administered to replenish fibrinogen in DIC? a) Whole blood b) Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) c) Cryoprecipitate d) Platelets Correct Answer: c) Cryoprecipitate Explanation: Cryoprecipitate is specifically used to replace fibrinogen levels, which are depleted in DIC. 23. Administering epinephrine counteracts histamine release, while steroids and antihistamines manage inflammation. Fluids help address hypotension caused by systemic vasodilation. Which approach is most effective in reducing the risk of nosocomial anemia in patients? a) Schedule routine laboratory tests at a fixed time daily b) Utilize low-volume blood collection tubes c) Extract blood samples through stopcocks d) Educate nursing assistants to draw blood samples Correct Answer: b) Utilize low-volume blood collection tubes Explanation: Using small-volume blood draw tubes minimizes blood loss during testing, reducing the risk of nosocomial anemia. A patient, one hour after delivering via abruptio placentae, exhibits pink-tinged urine and mild bleeding at the IV insertion site. Which laboratory evaluation is most appropriate at this time? a) Magnesium and oxytocin levels b) Factor X and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) c) Prothrombin time (PT) and D-dimer d) Creatinine and fibrin degradation products Correct Answer: c) Prothrombin time (PT) and D-dimer Explanation: This patient likely has disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) as a result of severe blood loss during delivery. PT and D-dimer tests can confirm this diagnosis by indicating coagulation dysfunction and fibrinolysis activity. Creatinine levels do not help diagnose DIC, and neither oxytocin nor magnesium levels are relevant in this context. 82.
