Serum osmolality is primarily affected by which of the following factors? a) Hemoglobin levels, serum potassium, and BUN b) Serum sodium, BUN, and glucose c) Serum calcium, glucose, and bicarbonate d) Albumin, serum chloride, and serum creatinine Correct Answer: b) Serum sodium, BUN, and glucose Explanation: Serum osmolality is calculated using sodium, BUN, and glucose. Which of the following osmolality values indicates a hypo-osmolar state? a) 290 mOsm/kg b) 280 mOsm/kg c) 270 mOsm/kg d) 300 mOsm/kg Correct Answer: c) 270 mOsm/kg Explanation: A hypo-osmolar state occurs when osmolality is less than 275 mOsm/kg. The hypothalamus regulates which of the following? a) Parathyroid gland hormone release b) Pancreatic hormone secretion c) Heart rate and oxygen saturation d) Temperature and intake drives Correct Answer: d) Temperature and intake drives Explanation: The hypothalamus regulates temperature, intake drives, and the autonomic nervous system. Values lower or higher indicate dilute or concentrated urine, respectively. 5. Which condition is characterized by increased ADH production, low urine output, and dilutional hyponatremia? a) Diabetes insipidus b) Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state c) Addison’s disease d) SIADH Correct Answer: d) SIADH Explanation: SIADH results from excessive ADH secretion, leading to water retention, low urine output, and dilutional hyponatremia with decreased serum osmolality. 6. Which of the following signs and symptoms are indicative of low blood sugar levels? a) Warm, dry skin and increased heart rate b) Slowed heart rate and excessive sweating c) Increased heart rate and muscle tremors d) Restlessness and warm, dry skin Correct Answer: c) Increased heart rate and muscle tremors Explanation: When blood glucose drops, it triggers the sympathetic nervous system, causing symptoms such as rapid heart rate and trembling. A patient presenting with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) has the following arterial blood gas (ABG) results on room air: pH: 7.22 PaCO₂: 21 mmHg pO₂: 94 mmHg HCO₃: 11 mmol/L What is the interpretation of the ABG? a) Uncompensated metabolic acidosis b) Compensated metabolic alkalosis c) Partially compensated metabolic acidosis d) Compensated respiratory acidosis Correct Answer: c) Partially compensated metabolic acidosis Explanation: A low pH indicates acidosis, and the low bicarbonate reflects a metabolic cause. The lungs attempt partial compensation by lowering PaCO₂ through hyperventilation.
