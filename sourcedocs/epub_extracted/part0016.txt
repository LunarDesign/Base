What is the primary mechanism responsible for maintaining blood pressure during the compensatory stage of shock? a) Increased cellular ATP production b) Anaerobic metabolism c) Increased lactate production d) Sympathetic nervous system stimulation and RAAS activation Correct Answer: d) Sympathetic nervous system stimulation and RAAS activation Explanation: During the compensatory stage of shock, blood pressure is maintained through the activation of the sympathetic nervous system (SNS), which causes vasoconstriction and increased heart rate, and the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), which retains sodium and water to support blood pressure. 2. Which of the following occurs as a result of inadequate tissue perfusion in shock? a) Aerobic metabolism predominates b) Anaerobic metabolism with increased lactate production c) Decreased lactate production d) Increased ATP production Correct Answer: b) Anaerobic metabolism with increased lactate production Explanation: Inadequate tissue perfusion leads to anaerobic metabolism due to insufficient oxygen delivery. This causes the production of lactate and hydrogen ions, resulting in acidosis. 3. What physiological response results from activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) in shock? a) Enhanced ATP production in cells b) Sodium and water retention, leading to maintained blood pressure c) Increased heart rate and contractility d) Decreased vasoconstriction and blood pressure Correct Answer: b) Sodium and water retention, leading to maintained blood pressure Explanation: RAAS activation leads to the release of aldosterone, which promotes sodium and water retention. This increases circulating volume and helps maintain blood pressure during the compensatory stage of shock. 4. In the compensatory stage of shock, which of the following is a direct effect of sympathetic nervous system stimulation? a) Increased heart rate and vasoconstriction b) Vasodilation of blood vessels c) Sodium and water excretion d) Decreased contractility of the heart Correct Answer: a) Increased heart rate and vasoconstriction Explanation: Sympathetic nervous system stimulation increases heart rate (tachycardia) and causes vasoconstriction to maintain blood pressure despite decreased cardiac output or circulating volume. 5. Which of the following is most indicative of the progressive stage of shock? a) BP is maintained through compensatory mechanisms b) Anaerobic metabolism has not yet occurred c) Cellular damage begins to occur, leading to acidosis d) Activation of the RAAS is effective in maintaining BP Correct Answer: c) Cellular damage begins to occur, leading to acidosis Explanation: In the progressive stage of shock, compensatory mechanisms fail, leading to worsening cellular hypoxia, anaerobic metabolism, and accumulation of lactate and hydrogen ions, which results in acidosis. 6. What happens when there is a failure to restore tissue oxygenation during shock? a) Acidosis develops, leading to cell death b) Aerobic metabolism prevents further tissue damage c) Increased ATP production and cell repair d) Lactate production decreases as oxygen becomes available Correct Answer: a) Acidosis develops, leading to cell death Explanation: Without restoration of tissue oxygenation, anaerobic metabolism continues, leading to acidosis, decreased ATP production, and eventually, cell death. 7. A nurse is evaluating a patient in shock. Which of the following findings suggests activation of the RAAS system? a) Vasodilation and increased cardiac output b) Sodium and water retention with decreased urine output c) Decreased blood pressure and increased lactate levels d) Increased urine output and decreased heart rate Correct Answer: b) Sodium and water retention with decreased urine output Explanation: RAAS activation leads to sodium and water retention to increase intravascular volume and maintain blood pressure. This process reduces urine output. 8. Which of the following best describes the compensatory stage of shock? a) Blood pressure and cardiac output normalize completely b) Cellular ATP production increases significantly c) Irreversible damage to cells with severe acidosis d) BP is maintained through physiological mechanisms despite hypoperfusion Correct Answer: d) BP is maintained through physiological mechanisms despite hypoperfusion Explanation: In the compensatory stage of shock, blood pressure is maintained through the activation of the sympathetic nervous system and RAAS, despite reduced cardiac output and perfusion. 9. What is the role of vasoconstriction during the compensatory stage of shock? a) To redirect blood flow to vital organs and maintain BP b) To increase ATP production in tissues c) To promote cellular repair and decrease oxygen demand d) To reduce cardiac workload by increasing blood flow to the kidneys Correct Answer: a) To redirect blood flow to vital organs and maintain BP Explanation: Vasoconstriction helps maintain blood pressure and redistributes blood flow to vital organs (heart, lungs, brain) during the compensatory stage of shock. 10. Which of the following statements about the RAAS system in shock is correct? a) Angiotensin II leads to vasodilation of blood vessels b) Renin secretion decreases as shock progresses c) RAAS activation helps maintain blood pressure via vasoconstriction and fluid retention d) Aldosterone release causes sodium and water excretion Correct Answer: c) RAAS activation helps maintain blood pressure via vasoconstriction and fluid retention Explanation: RAAS activation causes vasoconstriction (via angiotensin II) and aldosterone-mediated sodium and water retention to help maintain blood pressure in shock. 11. Which of the following clinical signs is most indicative of the compensatory stage of shock? a) Clammy and mottled skin with decreased LOC b) Multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) c) Tachycardia, tachypnea, and normal PaO₂ d) Hypotension and severe metabolic acidosis Correct Answer: c) Tachycardia, tachypnea, and normal PaO₂ Explanation: In the compensatory stage of shock, blood pressure is maintained through tachycardia and tachypnea. PaO₂ remains normal, and the patient may present with pale, cool skin, restlessness, and oliguria. 12. What is a key characteristic of the progressive stage of shock? a) Hypotension, metabolic acidosis, and decreased PaO₂ b) Skin that is pale, cool, and dry c) Normal blood pressure and PaO₂ d) Metabolic alkalosis and skin flushing Correct Answer: a) Hypotension, metabolic acidosis, and decreased PaO₂ Explanation: In the progressive stage, compensatory mechanisms fail, leading to hypotension, metabolic acidosis, worsening tachycardia, and decreased PaO₂. 13. Which of the following clinical findings is expected in the refractory stage of shock? a) Severe systemic hypoperfusion and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) b) Tachycardia with mild restlessness and normal skin temperature c) Normal right atrial pressure and cardiac output d) Blood pressure maintained through compensatory mechanisms Correct Answer: a) Severe systemic hypoperfusion and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) Explanation: The refractory stage involves severe systemic hypoperfusion, leading to MODS and failure of multiple organs such as lungs (ARDS), kidneys (ATN), heart, and liver. 14. Which organ is most likely to be affected in a patient with refractory shock leading to ARDS? a) Liver b) Heart c) Kidneys d) Lungs Correct Answer: d) Lungs Explanation: In refractory shock, severe systemic hypoperfusion leads to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), which is a failure of the lungs. 15. Which of the following is a hallmark of hypovolemic shock? a) Increased cardiac output and SvO₂ b) Increased right atrial pressure (CVP) c) Narrow pulse pressure with decreased cardiac output d) Decreased systemic vascular resistance (SVR) Correct Answer: c) Narrow pulse pressure with decreased cardiac output Explanation: Hypovolemic shock is characterized by decreased blood pressure, decreased cardiac output, and narrow pulse pressure due to reduced intravascular volume. 16. What happens to systemic vascular resistance (SVR) during hypovolemic shock? a) It increases to compensate for reduced cardiac output. b) It remains unchanged. c) It decreases due to vasodilation. d) It increases due to increased blood volume. Correct Answer: a) It increases to compensate for reduced cardiac output. Explanation: In hypovolemic shock, SVR increases as a compensatory mechanism to maintain blood pressure in response to decreased intravascular volume. 17. Which of the following hemodynamic changes is consistent with hypovolemic shock? a) Increased cardiac output and SvO₂ b) Decreased right atrial pressure (CVP) and cardiac output c) Decreased systemic vascular resistance (SVR) d) Increased left atrial pressure (PAOP) and BP Correct Answer: b) Decreased right atrial pressure (CVP) and cardiac output Explanation: In hypovolemic shock, right atrial pressure (CVP), cardiac output, and oxygen delivery decrease due to reduced circulating volume. 18. A patient in hypovolemic shock has a baseline blood pressure of 130/80. Which of the following readings suggests a narrowing pulse pressure? a) 110/80 b) 140/90 c) 120/90 d) 130/85 Correct Answer: a) 110/80 Explanation: Narrowing pulse pressure occurs when the systolic blood pressure decreases while the diastolic pressure remains constant or slightly increases, as seen in 110/80. 19. Which of the following is an internal cause of hypovolemic shock? a) GI losses b) Third-spacing or pooling in the intravascular compartment c) Burns d) Hemorrhage Correct Answer: b) Third-spacing or pooling in the intravascular compartment Explanation: Internal causes of hypovolemic shock include third-spacing or pooling of fluid within the intravascular compartment, leading to reduced circulating volume. 20. What type of shock occurs due to cardiac tamponade or tension pneumothorax? a) Obstructive shock b) Hypovolemic shock c) Cardiogenic shock d) Anaphylactic shock Correct Answer: a) Obstructive shock Explanation: Obstructive shock results from mechanical obstructions to blood flow, such as cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax, or massive pulmonary embolism. 21. Which of the following statements best describes anaphylactic shock? a) It results from a mechanical obstruction to blood flow. b) It is caused by loss of intravascular volume due to hemorrhage. c) It involves severe systemic vasodilation due to an immune response. d) It is caused by a critical reduction in cardiac output. Correct Answer: c) It involves severe systemic vasodilation due to an immune response. Explanation: Anaphylactic shock occurs due to severe systemic vasodilation triggered by an allergic or hypersensitivity reaction, leading to decreased tissue perfusion. 22. What is the most common cause of hypovolemic shock? a) Heart failure b) Anaphylaxis c) Critical reduction in intravascular volume d) Internal hemorrhage Correct Answer: c) Critical reduction in intravascular volume Explanation: Hypovolemic shock is caused by a critical reduction in circulating intravascular volume, most commonly due to hemorrhage, GI losses, or burns. 23. A patient presents with hypotension, tachycardia, oliguria, and pale, cool skin. Which stage of shock are they most likely experiencing? a) Compensatory stage b) Refractory stage c) Recovery stage d) Progressive stage Correct Answer: a) Compensatory stage Explanation: In the compensatory stage of shock, blood pressure is maintained through compensatory mechanisms, but the patient exhibits signs like tachycardia, tachypnea, oliguria, and pale, cool skin. 24. A patient with a massive pulmonary embolism is most likely experiencing which type of shock? a) Cardiogenic shock b) Neurogenic shock c) Hypovolemic shock d) Obstructive shock Correct Answer: d) Obstructive shock Explanation: A massive pulmonary embolism obstructs blood flow to the lungs, leading to obstructive shock. 25. What is the primary goal of fluid resuscitation in hypovolemic shock? a) To raise blood pressure with vasopressors b) To correct hyperchloremic acidosis c) To increase systemic vascular resistance (SVR) d) To sustain aerobic metabolism and maintain oxygen delivery to tissues Correct Answer: d) To sustain aerobic metabolism and maintain oxygen delivery to tissues Explanation: The goal of fluid resuscitation is to maintain oxygen delivery (DO₂) and oxygen uptake (VO₂) into tissues to sustain aerobic metabolism, thereby ensuring adequate perfusion. 26. Why are vasopressors avoided in the treatment of hypovolemic shock? a) The systemic vascular resistance (SVR) is already elevated due to compensatory mechanisms. b) They worsen fluid retention. c) They increase the risk of metabolic acidosis. d) They decrease urine output. Correct Answer: a) The systemic vascular resistance (SVR) is already elevated due to compensatory mechanisms. Explanation: In hypovolemic shock, SVR is already high as a compensatory response, so vasopressors are not indicated and may worsen tissue perfusion. 27. What is the recommended resuscitation fluid for trauma patients according to the ACS Committee on Trauma? a) Lactated Ringer’s b) Hypertonic saline c) Normal saline d) Colloids Correct Answer: a) Lactated Ringer’s Explanation: Lactated Ringer’s is recommended for trauma patients because it best mimics extracellular fluid (ECF) minus proteins. 28. Which of the following conditions is a contraindication for using lactated Ringer’s solution? a) Blood transfusion line use b) Hypernatremia c) Hyperchloremic acidosis d) Severe hypoperfusion Correct Answer: a) Blood transfusion line use Explanation: Lactated Ringer’s should not be given through a blood product transfusion line or to patients who cannot tolerate potassium or lactate. 29. A patient in Class III hemorrhagic shock has lost approximately how much blood? a) More than 2,000 mL b) Up to 750 mL c) 750-1,500 mL d) 1,500-2,000 mL Correct Answer: d) 1,500-2,000 mL Explanation: Class III hemorrhagic shock corresponds to a blood loss of 1,500-2,000 mL, representing approximately 30-40% of total blood volume. 30. What is the expected heart rate in a patient with Class II hemorrhagic shock? a) Greater than 140 bpm b) Between 100-120 bpm c) Less than 100 bpm d) Greater than 120 bpm Correct Answer: b) Between 100-120 bpm Explanation: In Class II hemorrhagic shock, the heart rate increases to greater than 100 bpm but typically does not exceed 120 bpm. 31. What urine output is expected in Class IV hemorrhagic shock? a) Greater than 30 mL/hr b) Between 5-15 mL/hr c) Between 20-30 mL/hr d) Scant or negligible urine output Correct Answer: d) Scant or negligible urine output Explanation: In Class IV hemorrhagic shock, urine output is scant due to severe hypoperfusion and decreased renal blood flow. 32. Which resuscitation approach is appropriate for a patient with Class III hemorrhagic shock? a) Vasopressors and crystalloids b) Crystalloids only c) Crystalloids and blood products d) Hypertonic saline Correct Answer: c) Crystalloids and blood products Explanation: Class III hemorrhagic shock involves 30-40% blood loss and requires resuscitation with crystalloids and blood products to restore perfusion. 33. Why is a fluid warmer recommended during hypovolemic shock management? a) To increase systemic vascular resistance (SVR) b) To prevent hypothermia during rapid fluid administration c) To reduce the risk of metabolic acidosis d) To accelerate the absorption of crystalloids Correct Answer: b) To prevent hypothermia during rapid fluid administration Explanation: A fluid warmer is used to prevent hypothermia when administering large volumes of fluids (> 2,000 mL/hour), especially in trauma patients. 34. Which of the following is a disadvantage of using normal saline for fluid resuscitation? a) It cannot be given to patients with hypernatremia. b) It contains calcium, which interferes with blood transfusion. c) It may cause hyperchloremic acidosis when large volumes are administered. d) It promotes potassium accumulation. Correct Answer: c) It may cause hyperchloremic acidosis when large volumes are administered. Explanation: Large volumes of normal saline can lead to hyperchloremic acidosis due to its high chloride content. 35. Which of the following resuscitation endpoints indicates improved tissue perfusion? a) Decreased systemic vascular resistance (SVR) b) Urine output of 0.5 mL/kg/hr c) Hemoglobin < 7.0 g/dL d) Mean arterial pressure (MAP) < 60 mmHg Correct Answer: b) Urine output of 0.5 mL/kg/hr Explanation: An adequate urine output of 0.5 mL/kg/hr indicates improved renal perfusion and overall tissue perfusion. 36. What happens to pulse pressure during Class II hemorrhagic shock? a) It widens. b) It decreases. c) It increases significantly. d) It remains normal. Correct Answer: b) It decreases. Explanation: Pulse pressure decreases during Class II hemorrhagic shock due to reduced stroke volume and compensatory vasoconstriction. 37. What is the typical respiratory rate seen in Class III hemorrhagic shock? a) 30-40 breaths/min b) Greater than 40 breaths/min c) 20-30 breaths/min d) 14-20 breaths/min Correct Answer: a) 30-40 breaths/min Explanation: In Class III hemorrhagic shock, the respiratory rate increases to 30-40 breaths/min as a compensatory mechanism for metabolic acidosis. 38. Which hemodynamic parameter is typically decreased in hypovolemic shock? a) Pulmonary artery occlusion pressure (PAOP) b) Cardiac output (CO) c) Mean arterial pressure (MAP) d) Systemic vascular resistance (SVR) Correct Answer: b) Cardiac output (CO) Explanation: In hypovolemic shock, cardiac output (CO) decreases due to reduced circulating volume, leading to inadequate tissue perfusion. 39. What is the primary goal when managing hemorrhagic shock? a) Initiate vasopressors to stabilize blood pressure b) Increase hemoglobin levels to >10 g/dL immediately c) Administer hypertonic fluids to restore volume d) Replace blood loss and stop the bleeding Correct Answer: d) Replace blood loss and stop the bleeding Explanation: The immediate goal in hemorrhagic shock is to stop bleeding and replace blood volume to restore tissue perfusion and oxygen delivery. 40. Which of the following transfusion products provides coagulation factors but no platelets? a) Packed red blood cells (PRBCs) b) Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) c) Platelets d) Cryoprecipitate Correct Answer: b) Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) Explanation: Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) contains coagulation factors but does not include platelets or red blood cells. 41. What is the main reason hemoglobin (Hgb) levels are unreliable during active bleeding? a) Blood transfusions distort measurements b) Hgb levels drop only in severe hemorrhage c) Loss of both plasma and red blood cells keeps the ratio unchanged initially d) Hemoglobin is replaced faster than plasma volume Correct Answer: c) Loss of both plasma and red blood cells keeps the ratio unchanged initially Explanation: During active bleeding, the loss of plasma and red blood cells occurs proportionally, keeping hemoglobin levels initially unchanged until fluid replacement dilutes the blood. 42. What is the consequence of hypothermia during blood transfusion in hemorrhagic shock? a) Citrate binds less ionized calcium (Ca++) b) Viral infection transmission worsens c) Impairment of red cell deformability and platelet dysfunction d) Hemolytic reactions increase Correct Answer: c) Impairment of red cell deformability and platelet dysfunction Explanation: Hypothermia impairs red cell deformability, reduces platelet function, and increases the affinity of hemoglobin to hold onto oxygen, worsening oxygen delivery. 43. Which of the following best describes the purpose of a massive transfusion protocol? a) To administer crystalloids and colloids to restore blood pressure b) To rapidly infuse blood products and restore oxygen delivery and perfusion c) To replace hemoglobin levels above 10 g/dL d) To prevent immune-related transfusion reactions Correct Answer: b) To rapidly infuse blood products and restore oxygen delivery and perfusion Explanation: Massive transfusion protocols ensure rapid administration of blood products to restore oxygen delivery, tissue perfusion, and blood pressure during significant blood loss. 44. What is the definition of massive transfusion? a) 7 units of RBCs within 12 hours b) 10 units of RBCs within 24 hours or 5 units in less than 3 hours c) 5 units of RBCs over 6 hours d) 15 units of RBCs within 48 hours Correct Answer: b) 10 units of RBCs within 24 hours or 5 units in less than 3 hours Explanation: A massive transfusion is defined as the infusion of 10 units of RBCs in 24 hours or 5 units within less than 3 hours. 45. What is the consequence of low 2,3-DPG levels in stored blood products? a) Reduced blood clotting factors b) Risk of transfusion-related acute lung injury (TRALI) c) Reduced platelet function d) Increased affinity of hemoglobin for oxygen Correct Answer: d) Increased affinity of hemoglobin for oxygen Explanation: Low 2,3-DPG levels shift the oxyhemoglobin-dissociation curve to the left, increasing hemoglobin’s affinity for oxygen and reducing oxygen release to tissues. 46. Which of the following conditions is NOT associated with the triad of death in massive transfusion? a) Hypothermia b) Hypernatremia c) Acidosis d) Coagulopathy Correct Answer: b) Hypernatremia Explanation: The triad of death in massive transfusion includes hypothermia, acidosis, and coagulopathy, which together worsen outcomes in hemorrhagic shock. 47. Why is hypocalcemia a concern during blood transfusions? a) Packed red blood cells are deficient in calcium. b) Calcium competes with potassium during transfusions. c) Citrate in transfused blood binds to ionized calcium. d) Coagulation factors are destroyed by calcium. Explanation: Citrate used as a preservative in blood products binds to ionized calcium, leading to hypocalcemia if not monitored during massive transfusions. 48. Which blood product is administered specifically to replace fibrinogen? a) Platelets b) Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) c) Cryoprecipitate d) Packed red blood cells (PRBCs) Correct Answer: c) Cryoprecipitate Explanation: Cryoprecipitate is rich in fibrinogen and is administered to patients with low fibrinogen levels, often in hemorrhagic shock. 49. What is a major risk associated with transfusion-related acute lung injury (TRALI)? a) Increased likelihood of viral infections b) Pulmonary edema and respiratory distress c) Citrate toxicity leading to hypocalcemia d) Hyperchloremic acidosis Correct Answer: b) Pulmonary edema and respiratory distress Explanation: TRALI is a serious complication of blood transfusion characterized by noncardiogenic pulmonary edema and respiratory distress. 50. What clinical endpoint indicates adequate resuscitation in hemorrhagic shock? a) Hemoglobin > 10 g/dL b) Heart rate > 120 bpm c) Urine output > 0.5 mL/kg/hr d) Mean arterial pressure (MAP) < 60 mmHg Correct Answer: c) Urine output > 0.5 mL/kg/hr Explanation: Adequate urine output (> 0.5 mL/kg/hr) reflects improved renal perfusion and sufficient resuscitation in hemorrhagic shock. 51. Which of the following interventions is most emphasized in the recent protocols for managing sepsis and septic shock? a) Continuous monitoring of ScvO₂ levels b) Measurement of central venous pressure (CVP) c) Immediate fluid resuscitation and timely antibiotics d) Routine measurement of arterial blood gases Correct Answer: c) Immediate fluid resuscitation and timely antibiotics Explanation: Current evidence-based protocols prioritize fluid resuscitation, timely administration of antibiotics, and lactate measurement over older practices like CVP and ScvO₂ monitoring. 52. What key clinical parameter is frequently measured to assess the severity of sepsis and guide treatment? a) Hemoglobin level b) Serum creatinine level c) Blood glucose level d) Serum lactate level Correct Answer: d) Serum lactate level Explanation: Serum lactate levels are critical in assessing tissue hypoperfusion and severity of sepsis, making them a key component of sepsis management protocols. 55. What is the definition of sepsis according to the Sepsis-3 criteria? a) Presence of an infection with organ dysfunction b) Presence of an infection with systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) c) Infection with markers of hypoperfusion requiring pressors d) Systemic inflammatory response without proven infection Correct Answer: a) Presence of an infection with organ dysfunction Explanation: The Sepsis-3 definition identifies sepsis as the presence of an infection combined with organ dysfunction, replacing the older SIRS-based definition. 65.
