part0012 6. Integumentary 1. A patient with diabetes and peripheral vascular disease presents with a complex wound. Which of the following factors most likely complicates the healing process of this wound? a) Poor nutritional status b) Adequate nutrition c) Normal blood glucose levels d) Regular exercise Correct Answer: a) Poor nutritional status Explanation: Poor nutritional status is a key factor that complicates wound healing, especially in patients with comorbidities like diabetes and peripheral vascular disease. Nutrient deficiencies can impair tissue repair and immune function, hindering the healing of complex wounds. 2. Which of the following is the most common cause of a pressure injury (PI)? a) Immobility and pressure on bony prominences b) Infection and diabetes c) Inadequate hydration d) Poor nutritional intake Correct Answer: a) Immobility and pressure on bony prominences Explanation: Pressure injuries most commonly occur due to sustained pressure on bony prominences, especially in immobile patients. This pressure reduces blood flow to the skin and underlying tissues, leading to tissue damage. 3. When assessing a patient for the presence of a pressure injury upon admission, which action should the nurse take? a) Avoid using a risk assessment tool b) Perform a deep tissue inspection immediately c) Assess the skin surfaces each shift d) Only assess the lower extremities Correct Answer: c) Assess the skin surfaces each shift Explanation: It is important to assess the skin surfaces each shift to detect any pressure injuries early, especially upon admission. Routine assessment helps identify potential injuries and prevents further complications. 4. Which of the following is an important strategy to prevent pressure injuries in high-risk patients? a) Encouraging prolonged immobility b) Using diapers for incontinence management c) Limiting fluid intake to prevent swelling d) Repositioning the patient every 2 hours Correct Answer: d) Repositioning the patient every 2 hours Explanation: Repositioning the patient every 2 hours helps relieve pressure on bony prominences, a critical measure in preventing pressure injuries. Maintaining skin integrity through regular movement reduces the risk of pressure ulcers. 5. A patient with a pressure injury requires repositioning. Which device should the nurse use to prevent shearing/friction while moving the patient? a) Pull sheets b) Lateral transfer devices c) Diapers d) Standard hospital bed linens Correct Answer: b) Lateral transfer devices Explanation: Lateral transfer devices, such as sliding sheets, are designed to reduce shearing and friction when moving a patient. Pulling sheets increases the risk of skin damage and should be avoided in patients with pressure injuries. 6. Which of the following conditions is considered a risk factor for developing a pressure injury? a) Decreased perfusion b) Normal hydration levels c) Good nutritional status d) Increased mobility Correct Answer: a) Decreased perfusion Explanation: Decreased perfusion, which can occur in conditions like peripheral vascular disease, is a significant risk factor for the development of pressure injuries. Poor blood flow impairs the delivery of oxygen and nutrients to tissues, increasing the risk of tissue damage. 7. What should the nurse do when a patient has fragile skin and requires securing a medical device? a) Use adhesive tape b) Avoid securing medical devices c) Secure the device with cloth tape d) Use paper tape for those with fragile skin Correct Answer: d) Use paper tape for those with fragile skin Explanation: Paper tape is gentler on fragile skin than standard adhesive tape, reducing the risk of skin tears or further injury while still securely holding medical devices in place. 8. A patient is at high risk for developing a pressure injury due to immobility, poor nutritional status, and hypoxemia. What is the first priority intervention to prevent the injury? a) Administer sedatives to aid in relaxation b) Provide pressure-relieving surfaces c) Limit patient mobility d) Increase fluid intake Correct Answer: b) Provide pressure-relieving surfaces Explanation: The first priority in preventing pressure injuries in high-risk patients is providing pressure-relieving surfaces, such as special mattresses or seat cushions. These surfaces help distribute pressure and reduce the risk of skin breakdown. 9. A nurse is assessing a patient for signs of a pressure injury. Which of the following should be documented as a common sign of a pressure injury? a) Soft-tissue injuries b) Frequent visits to the emergency department c) Lack of personal hygiene d) Pain and edema in the extremities Correct Answer: a) Soft-tissue injuries Explanation: Soft-tissue injuries, such as redness, blanching, or breakdown of the skin, are common signs of a pressure injury. These injuries occur due to sustained pressure and can lead to deeper tissue damage if not addressed. 10. What is the primary purpose of using a risk assessment tool, such as the Braden or Norton Scale, in patients at risk for pressure injuries? a) To monitor the effectiveness of wound care interventions b) To identify patients at high risk for developing pressure injuries c) To assess the patient's pain levels d) To determine the need for surgical intervention Correct Answer: b) To identify patients at high risk for developing pressure injuries Explanation: The Braden and Norton scales are used to assess and identify patients at high risk for developing pressure injuries by evaluating factors such as mobility, nutrition, moisture, and perfusion. Early identification helps guide preventive care strategies. 11. When assessing a pressure injury (PI) upon admission, the nurse observes redness in a localized area with intact skin. What stage is this pressure injury classified as? a) Stage 3 b) Stage 2 c) Stage 1 d) Stage 4 Correct Answer: c) Stage 1 Explanation: Stage 1 pressure injuries are characterized by intact skin with localized redness. This is the earliest stage of pressure injury, and the skin remains intact without any open areas or loss of tissue. 12. A patient presents with a wound that has partial thickness skin loss with exposed dermis. The wound bed is pink or red and moist, and a blister-like appearance is noted. What stage of pressure injury is this? a) Stage 3 b) Stage 1 c) Stage 2 d) Stage 4 Correct Answer: c) Stage 2 Explanation: Stage 2 pressure injuries are characterized by partial thickness skin loss, exposing the dermis. The wound bed is usually pink or red and moist, and it may present as a blister. 13. A patient has a wound with full thickness skin loss, and the subcutaneous fat is visible. What stage is this pressure injury classified as? a) Stage 4 b) Stage 2 c) Stage 3 d) Stage 1 Correct Answer: c) Stage 3 Explanation: Stage 3 pressure injuries involve full thickness skin loss, where subcutaneous fat becomes visible. This stage indicates a deeper wound, but fascia, bone, and muscle are not exposed. 14. A pressure injury with exposed bone, tendon, or muscle is classified as which stage? a) Stage 2 b) Stage 1 c) Stage 4 d) Stage 3 Correct Answer: c) Stage 4 Explanation: Stage 4 pressure injuries are characterized by full thickness skin loss with exposure or palpable fascia, bone, tendon, ligament, or muscle. Slough or eschar may also be present. 15. A patient presents with a pressure injury where the extent of tissue damage is unclear due to slough or eschar obscuring the wound. What classification does this pressure injury receive? a) Stage 1 b) Stage 3 c) Stage 2 d) Unstageable Correct Answer: d) Unstageable Explanation: A pressure injury is classified as unstageable when there is full-thickness tissue loss, but the extent of damage cannot be determined due to slough or eschar obscuring the wound. Once the eschar or slough is removed, the injury may then be classified. 16. When a pressure injury is identified, what is the first action the nurse should take? a) Apply antibiotic ointment immediately b) Perform wound debridement c) Reposition the patient every 2 hours d) Notify the provider and request a wound care consult Correct Answer: d) Notify the provider and request a wound care consult Explanation: The first step in managing a pressure injury is to notify the provider and request a wound care consult. This allows for a detailed treatment plan to be discussed and ensures appropriate care is provided. 17. Which of the following strategies should the nurse use to relieve pressure over bony prominences in a patient with a pressure injury? a) Limit movement to avoid further injury b) Reposition the patient every 4 hours c) Apply pressure-reducing surfaces d) Keep the head of the bed at a 45° angle Correct Answer: c) Apply pressure-reducing surfaces Explanation: Pressure-reducing surfaces, such as special mattresses or seat cushions, help relieve pressure over bony prominences and are essential in managing pressure injuries. Repositioning the patient regularly is also important. 18. The patient with a pressure injury is receiving wound debridement. Which of the following methods is considered enzymatic debridement? a) Forceful irrigation b) Proteolytic enzymes c) Surgical excision d) Wet-to-dry dressings Correct Answer: b) Proteolytic enzymes Explanation: Enzymatic debridement uses proteolytic enzymes to break down necrotic tissue in the wound. This method is effective in promoting wound healing while removing damaged tissue. 19. A patient’s pressure injury requires debridement. Which of the following is an example of nonselective mechanical debridement? a) Hydrocolloid dressings b) Whirlpool treatments c) Proteolytic enzymes d) Surgical debridement Correct Answer: b) Whirlpool treatments Explanation: Nonselective mechanical debridement includes methods like whirlpool treatments, which use water to help loosen and remove dead tissue. These methods may also include forceful irrigation or wet-to-dry dressings. 20. What is an important aspect of care when managing a pressure injury with a wound VAC (Vacuum Assisted Closure) system? a) Ensure the system is functioning properly as ordered by the provider b) Use standard dressings over the wound site c) Avoid repositioning the patient to prevent pressure on the wound d) Keep the wound exposed to air Correct Answer: a) Ensure the system is functioning properly as ordered by the provider Explanation: A wound VAC system helps promote healing by applying negative pressure to the wound. It is important to ensure the system is functioning properly as ordered by the provider to facilitate proper wound healing and tissue regeneration. 21. A wound VAC system is used to promote healing in complex wounds. What is the primary mechanism through which negative-pressure wound therapy aids in wound healing? a) It increases blood flow to the area b) It prevents infection by killing bacteria c) It reduces pain and swelling d) It removes necrotic tissue from the wound Correct Answer: a) It increases blood flow to the area Explanation: Negative-pressure wound therapy promotes healing by applying continuous or intermittent negative pressure, which increases blood flow to the affected area, enhancing tissue perfusion and encouraging wound healing. 22. The nurse is managing a patient on wound VAC therapy. What should the nurse do if the system alarms due to a full canister? a) Disconnect the system and restart it later b) Ignore the alarm if the patient is stable c) Apply a new dressing and continue monitoring without addressing the alarm d) Change the evacuation canister as soon as possible Correct Answer: d) Change the evacuation canister as soon as possible Explanation: When the canister is full, it should be changed according to the manufacturer’s guidelines, typically once a week or earlier if necessary. Ignoring the alarm or not addressing it promptly could disrupt the wound healing process. 23. What is the maximum time that wound VAC therapy can be safely interrupted without increasing the patient’s risk of infection? a) 1 hour b) 2 hours c) 3 hours d) 4 hours Correct Answer: b) 2 hours Explanation: If the wound VAC therapy is interrupted for longer than 2 hours, the patient’s risk for infection increases. If interruption is necessary, a wet-to-damp dressing should be used until therapy can resume. 24. A patient with necrotizing fasciitis presents with intense pain, redness, and purplish discoloration of the skin. What is the most appropriate initial intervention? a) Start oral antibiotics and wait for results from blood cultures b) Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling c) Administer a pain-relieving analgesic and monitor the wound d) Initiate immediate surgical debridement Correct Answer: d) Initiate immediate surgical debridement Explanation: Necrotizing fasciitis is a surgical emergency. Early and aggressive surgical debridement is crucial to stop the spread of necrosis and reduce morbidity and mortality. Antibiotic therapy and other supportive treatments will follow. 25. Which of the following is NOT a common risk factor for developing necrotizing fasciitis? a) Recent surgery b) HIV infection c) Diabetes d) Hyperthyroidism Correct Answer: d) Hyperthyroidism Explanation: Risk factors for necrotizing fasciitis include immunocompromised states such as diabetes, cancer, HIV infection, and recent surgery or trauma. Hyperthyroidism is not typically a risk factor for this condition. 26. A patient with necrotizing fasciitis develops fever, malaise, and systemic signs of infection. What is the next most important step in management? a) Continue monitoring without intervention b) Treat hemodynamic instability with fluids and pressors c) Start hyperbaric oxygen therapy d) Provide nutritional support immediately Correct Answer: b) Treat hemodynamic instability with fluids and pressors Explanation: Hemodynamic instability, such as hypotension or shock, should be treated immediately with fluids and vasopressors. This is a critical step to support circulation and organ perfusion in patients with necrotizing fasciitis. 27. What is the recommended initial antibiotic approach in managing necrotizing fasciitis before blood culture results are available? a) A single antibiotic targeting Gram-negative bacteria b) Broad-spectrum antibiotics based on the suspected causative organisms c) No antibiotics until the infection source is identified d) Antifungal therapy due to the risk of fungal infection Correct Answer: b) Broad-spectrum antibiotics based on the suspected causative organisms Explanation: Initial treatment of necrotizing fasciitis involves broad-spectrum antibiotics to cover a variety of potential pathogens. Once blood culture results are available, the antibiotic regimen can be adjusted accordingly. 28. A patient with necrotizing fasciitis has been started on aggressive debridement and antibiotic therapy. What additional treatment may be considered for some patients? a) Encouraging early ambulation to prevent blood clots b) Application of a heat pack to promote blood flow c) Use of corticosteroids to reduce inflammation d) Administration of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) Correct Answer: d) Administration of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) Explanation: Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) may be considered in select cases of necrotizing fasciitis to improve immune function and help control the infection. It is used in conjunction with other treatments such as surgery and antibiotics. 29. A fecal management system (FMS) is used for a patient with involuntary liquid diarrhea to prevent complications. Which of the following is a contraindication for the use of an internal FMS? a) Presence of liquid stool b) Recent lower large bowel surgery c) Patient with an intact rectal lining d) Patient with a history of hemorrhoids Correct Answer: b) Recent lower large bowel surgery Explanation: An internal fecal management system is contraindicated in patients who have recently had lower large bowel surgery, as it can interfere with healing or cause additional injury to the surgical site. 30. What is a key consideration when using an external fecal management system (FMS)? a) Frequent monitoring of the patient’s oral intake is required. b) It is used when the internal FMS cannot be used. c) The balloon catheter should be inflated before application. d) It should be applied to excoriated skin. Correct Answer: b) It is used when the internal FMS cannot be used. Explanation: The external FMS is indicated when the internal device cannot be used due to contraindications such as rectal injury or recent surgery. It is not applied over excoriated skin. 31. If an intravenous (IV) infiltration occurs, what should the nurse do first? a) Change the IV site and restart the infusion b) Stop the infusion immediately c) Continue the infusion at a slower rate d) Apply cold compress to the site Correct Answer: b) Stop the infusion immediately Explanation: Infiltration should be managed by immediately stopping the infusion to prevent further leakage of the medication or solution into the surrounding tissue, which could cause tissue damage. 32. A nurse is assessing an infiltration using an infiltration scale. A grade 4 infiltration is characterized by: a) Tight skin with deep pitting edema and moderate pain b) Edema of 1–6 inches with possible numbness c) Blanched and translucent skin with edema > 6 inches d) Edema < 1 inch and blanching of the skin Correct Answer: a) Tight skin with deep pitting edema and moderate pain Explanation: Grade 4 infiltration involves severe symptoms, including tight skin, deep pitting edema, moderate to severe pain, and possible extravasation of vesicants or irritants, which may cause tissue damage. 33. A nurse is caring for a patient with phlebitis. Which of the following is an appropriate action? a) Continue the IV infusion and monitor for changes b) Identify the cause of the reaction and address it as possible c) Elevate the extremity and apply cold compresses d) Start a new IV on the same extremity Correct Answer: b) Identify the cause of the reaction and address it as possible Explanation: The nurse should identify the cause of the phlebitis (e.g., irritant drug, mechanical device) and address it, stop the infusion, elevate the extremity, and restart the IV if necessary. Cold compresses are not recommended for phlebitis; warm compresses are more appropriate. 34. Which of the following strategies is most effective in preventing phlebitis during IV therapy? a) Choose a catheter size based on the patient's vein size b) Use highly concentrated drugs in the peripheral veins c) Use the largest gauge catheter available for infusion d) Avoid placing the catheter near joints or vein valves Correct Answer: d) Avoid placing the catheter near joints or vein valves Explanation: Avoiding placement of the catheter near joints or vein valves can reduce the risk of phlebitis, as these areas are more prone to irritation and mechanical stress from the catheter. Smaller, appropriately sized catheters should be used to prevent phlebitis. 35. In managing a patient with an infiltration graded as 4 on the infiltration scale, what should be done next? a) Administer pain medication immediately b) Elevate the extremity and apply a warm, moist compress c) Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling d) Monitor the site and continue the infusion with caution Correct Answer: b) Elevate the extremity and apply a warm, moist compress Explanation: For a grade 4 infiltration, the nurse should elevate the extremity and apply a warm, moist compress to promote resorption of the infiltrated solution and reduce discomfort. A cold compress is not recommended as it could worsen the situation. 36. What is the primary concern when extravasation of a vesicant drug occurs? a) It leads to increased blood flow and promotes healing b) It causes only temporary swelling without long-term effects c) It results in mild discomfort and requires monitoring d) It may cause severe tissue injury and necrosis Correct Answer: d) It may cause severe tissue injury and necrosis Explanation: Extravasation of a vesicant drug can lead to severe and irreversible tissue injury, including necrosis, blisters, pain, and tissue sloughing. It requires prompt action to prevent further damage. 37. What is the appropriate first step when extravasation occurs due to a vesicant drug? a) Start a new IV infusion on the same extremity b) Stop the infusion and consult with a pharmacist c) Apply warm compresses immediately d) Continue the infusion and monitor the site for changes Correct Answer: b) Stop the infusion and consult with a pharmacist Explanation: The first step in managing extravasation is to stop the infusion immediately and consult with a pharmacist to determine the appropriate antidote and management strategy, as vesicants require specific treatment to prevent further tissue damage. 38. Which of the following is typically used as an antidote for non-cytotoxic agent extravasations? a) Phentolamine (Regitine) b) Hyaluronidase c) Terbutaline d) Nitroglycerin ointment Correct Answer: b) Hyaluronidase Explanation: Hyaluronidase is commonly used as an antidote for non-cytotoxic agent extravasations (such as amiodarone or calcium gluconate) and some cytotoxic agents. It helps to break down the substance and reduce tissue damage. 39. When managing extravasation caused by vasopressors like norepinephrine, which antidote is typically used? a) Nitroglycerin ointment b) Calcium gluconate c) Hyaluronidase d) Phentolamine (Regitine) Correct Answer: d) Phentolamine (Regitine) Explanation: Phentolamine (Regitine) is used to treat extravasation of vasopressors like norepinephrine and dopamine. It works by reversing the vasoconstrictive effects and reducing the risk of tissue necrosis. 40. When managing an extravasation of a vesicant agent, which type of compress is usually indicated? a) Cold compress only for cytotoxic agents b) Cold compress for all agents c) Cold compress for most agents, warm compress if hyaluronidase or phentolamine is used d) Warm compress for all cases Correct Answer: c) Cold compress for most agents, warm compress if hyaluronidase or phentolamine is used Explanation: Cold compresses are typically used to treat extravasation of most vesicants, except when specific antidotes like hyaluronidase or phentolamine are used, in which case warm compresses are indicated to help facilitate the antidote's action. 41. A patient with a C. difficile infection is experiencing severe, uncontrollable diarrhea and has developed irritated skin with blisters on the buttocks and coccyx. What is the most suitable intervention for this patient? a) Maintain cleanliness and dryness in the affected area, applying a diaper along with a barrier cream. b) Obtain a prescription for the insertion of a fecal management system (FMS). c) Keep the area clean and dry, and apply an external fecal collection device. d) Consult the provider to administer an anti-diarrheal drug and request a wound care consultation. Correct Answer: b) Obtain a prescription for the insertion of a fecal management system (FMS). Explanation: The skin damage involves a bony area with blisters, indicating a Stage 2 pressure injury (PI). An internal fecal management system (FMS) can help prevent further irritation. Diapers (a) are not recommended for this purpose, and external fecal collection devices (c) are not suitable for excoriated skin. Anti-diarrheal medication (d) is inappropriate for a C. difficile infection, and wound care consultation is typically not needed for partial-thickness pressure injuries. 42. A patient is receiving a norepinephrine infusion through an intravenous line in the left antecubital area. The skin around the IV site appears swollen, red, and tender about half an inch above the insertion point. What should be the primary intervention for this patient? a) Apply cool compresses to the affected area. b) Administer hyaluronidase and use cold compresses. c) Remove the IV and restart it with a smaller catheter in the opposite arm. d) Administer phentolamine (Regitine) and apply warm compresses. Correct Answer: d) Administer phentolamine (Regitine) and apply warm compresses. Explanation: The patient is showing signs of a vesicant extravasation (Grade 1), which requires immediate administration of phentolamine (Regitine) and warm compresses to mitigate tissue damage. Cool compresses (a) and hyaluronidase (b) are not appropriate in this case, and restarting the IV (c) will not address the extravasation. 43. What is the priority treatment for a patient diagnosed with necrotizing fasciitis? a) Surgical removal of the necrotic tissue. b) Providing nutritional support. c) Hyperbaric oxygen therapy. d) Topical application of antibiotic ointment. Correct Answer: a) Surgical removal of the necrotic tissue. Explanation: Early surgical debridement of necrotic tissue is the most crucial step in managing necrotizing fasciitis, as it reduces the risk of further complications. While nutritional support (b) is important, it is not effective without addressing the necrotic tissue. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (c) may have benefits but is not the primary treatment. Topical antibiotics (d) are not sufficient for treating necrotizing fasciitis. 44. A trauma patient’s arterial blood gas reveals pH 7.48, pCO₂ 28 mmHg, pO₂ 68 mmHg, and HCO₃ 25 mmol/L. What intervention is most suitable? a) Administer sedation and monitor blood glucose b) Evaluate blood pressure and consider intubation c) Adjust FiO₂ and check electrolytes d) Increase FiO₂ and assess for pain Correct Answer: d) Increase FiO₂ and assess for pain Explanation: The ABG indicates respiratory alkalosis likely due to hypoxemia and pain. Pain management and oxygen supplementation can address these causes. Intubation and blood pressure adjustments are unnecessary as hyperventilation, not hypoventilation, is present. 45. For a patient receiving negative pressure wound therapy, which action is appropriate? a) Regularly flush the tubing b) Notify the provider if suction stops for over 4 hours c) Replace the dressing daily d) Change the canister when full or as recommended Correct Answer: d) Change the canister when full or as recommended Explanation: The canister must be replaced when full or per guidelines. Contacting the provider is necessary if suction is lost for over 2 hours, not 4. Dressing and tubing maintenance have specific recommendations not covered in the incorrect options. 46. A patient receiving IV fluids develops a 2-inch diameter swelling, blanched skin, and no pain at the IV site. What is the appropriate response in addition to stopping the infusion? a) Determine it as stage 1 infiltration and assess for blood return b) Consider it extravasation and consult the pharmacist for an antidote c) Classify it as a stage 2 infiltration and restart the IV in the opposite arm d) Identify it as a stage 0 infiltration, elevate the arm, and use warm compresses Correct Answer: c) Classify it as a stage 2 infiltration and restart the IV in the opposite arm Explanation: Stage 2 infiltration includes swelling of 1 to 6 inches and blanched skin. Restarting the IV in another extremity is necessary. It does not qualify as extravasation since the infused fluid is non-vesicant. 48. Which of the following best describes a pressure injury? a) A lip ulcer following endotracheal tube placement b) A skin tear due to IV dressing removal c) A skin abrasion after a fall d) A diabetic venous ulcer on the lower limb Correct Answer: a) A lip ulcer following endotracheal tube placement Explanation: Pressure injuries arise from prolonged pressure, such as a lip ulcer caused by an endotracheal tube. Other options, such as skin tears or diabetic ulcers, are not caused by pressure and thus do not qualify as pressure injuries. 49. A 21-year-old woman is being treated for necrotizing fasciitis in her left arm following an insect bite. Which statement about treatment is accurate? a) Immediate hyperbaric oxygen therapy is mandatory b) Vasopressors should never be administered c) Vancomycin is the preferred antibiotic d) Amputation may be necessary to control the infection Correct Answer: d) Amputation may be necessary to control the infection Explanation: In severe cases where necrotizing fasciitis does not respond to debridement, life-saving amputation may be required. Vancomycin is not universally the first-line antibiotic. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is beneficial but not always essential. Vasopressors are used in septic shock if necessary. 50. If potassium chloride infiltrates into a patient’s arm, what is the correct nursing intervention? a) Apply phentolamine with warm compresses. b) Inject hyaluronidase and use warm compresses. c) Administer hyaluronidase and use cold compresses. d) Administer phentolamine and apply cold compresses. Correct Answer: b) Inject hyaluronidase and use warm compresses. Explanation: Potassium chloride extravasation is treated with hyaluronidase and warm compresses to promote absorption and fluid dispersal. Phentolamine is not indicated for potassium chloride infiltration, and cold compresses are not used with hyaluronidase. 51. Other options are less effective in addressing this condition. 52. Which critically ill patient has the highest risk of developing pressure injuries? a) A patient receiving mechanical ventilation b) A 66-year-old patient on three vasopressors c) A patient diagnosed with Guillain-Barré syndrome d) An 81-year-old patient with an acute anterior myocardial infarction Correct Answer: b) A 66-year-old patient on three vasopressors Explanation: Vasopressors compromise skin perfusion, heightening the likelihood of rapid pressure injury development compared to other scenarios.
