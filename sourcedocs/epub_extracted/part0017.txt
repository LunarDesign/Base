Which of the following is the most common type of delirium observed in critically ill patients? a) Purely hyperactive delirium b) Hypoactive delirium c) Delirium superimposed on dementia d) Mixed delirium Correct Answer: d) Mixed delirium Explanation: Mixed delirium, which includes both hyperactive and hypoactive features, is the most common type and is seen in approximately 55% of patients with delirium. 2. A patient in the ICU with dementia develops an acute fluctuating impairment in cognition. What condition is this patient most likely experiencing? a) Sensory overload b) Hypoactive delirium c) Delirium superimposed on dementia d) Purely hyperactive delirium Correct Answer: c) Delirium superimposed on dementia Explanation: Patients with a history of dementia can develop delirium superimposed on dementia, characterized by acute and reversible changes in mental status. 3. Which of the following risk factors for delirium is considered modifiable? a) Greater age b) History of dementia c) Increasing APACHE score d) Immobility Correct Answer: d) Immobility Explanation: Immobility is a modifiable risk factor for delirium, meaning interventions like early mobilization can reduce its occurrence. Non-modifiable factors include history of dementia, advanced age, and increasing APACHE score. 4. Which tool is specifically validated to assess delirium in critically ill patients in the ICU? a) Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU (CAM-ICU) b) Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) c) Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) d) Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS) Correct Answer: a) Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU (CAM-ICU) Explanation: The CAM-ICU is a validated and reliable tool specifically designed to identify delirium in ICU patients. The ICDSC is another validated option for delirium assessment. 5. Which of the following factors can increase a patient’s risk of developing delirium in the ICU? a) Frequent sensory stimulation b) High Glasgow Coma Scale score c) Administration of benzodiazepines d) Absence of prior coma Correct Answer: c) Administration of benzodiazepines Explanation: Benzodiazepines are a modifiable risk factor for delirium, as their use has been associated with its development. Strategies such as minimizing sedation and using alternative agents can help reduce this risk. 6. What is the second most common type of delirium seen in ICU patients? a) Delirium due to substance withdrawal b) Purely hyperactive delirium c) Mixed delirium d) Hypoactive delirium Correct Answer: d) Hypoactive delirium Explanation: Hypoactive delirium is the second most common type, seen in approximately 43% of patients with delirium. It is often underrecognized due to its subtle presentation. 7. A nurse is unable to identify hypoactive delirium in an ICU patient. What is the most likely explanation for this challenge? a) Hypoactive delirium is associated with severe agitation. b) Hypoactive delirium presents with subtle, less obvious symptoms. c) Assessment tools do not detect hypoactive delirium effectively. d) Nurses are not trained to use delirium screening tools. Correct Answer: b) Hypoactive delirium presents with subtle, less obvious symptoms. Explanation: Hypoactive delirium is often overlooked because its symptoms, such as reduced movement and lethargy, are subtle and less obvious compared to hyperactive delirium. 8. Which of the following strategies can help reduce modifiable risk factors for delirium in ICU patients? a) Increase blood transfusion thresholds b) Implement early mobilization protocols c) Apply physical restraints routinely d) Initiate benzodiazepine therapy early Correct Answer: b) Implement early mobilization protocols Explanation: Early mobilization is a strategy that addresses immobility, a modifiable risk factor for delirium. Other strategies include reducing benzodiazepine use and optimizing pain management. 9. A patient in the ICU exhibits fluctuating mental status with sudden episodes of restlessness and lethargy. What type of delirium is most likely present? a) Hypoactive delirium b) Purely hyperactive delirium c) Substance withdrawal delirium d) Mixed delirium Correct Answer: d) Mixed delirium Explanation: Mixed delirium is characterized by alternating hyperactive (restlessness) and hypoactive (lethargy) symptoms and is the most common form of delirium in critically ill patients. 10. Why is it important to identify and assess delirium in ICU patients? a) Delirium increases cognitive recovery and patient satisfaction. b) Delirium is only associated with hyperactive symptoms. c) Delirium has minimal impact on patient mortality. d) Delirium is associated with increased ICU length of stay and costs. Correct Answer: d) Delirium is associated with increased ICU length of stay and costs. Explanation: Delirium is associated with increased ICU and hospital length of stay, higher healthcare costs, worse cognitive outcomes, and increased patient/family distress. Which of the following is the first step in assessing a patient for delirium? a) Determining disorganized thinking b) Evaluating for inattention c) Checking for acute onset of mental status changes or a fluctuating course d) Assessing altered level of consciousness Correct Answer: c) Checking for acute onset of mental status changes or a fluctuating course Explanation: The first step in delirium assessment is checking for acute onset of mental status changes or a fluctuating course. If this criterion is negative, the patient is not assessed further for delirium. 12. When should the assessment for delirium be stopped immediately? a) If the patient is negative for acute onset of mental status changes or fluctuating course b) If the patient has a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of 15 c) If the patient is positive for inattention d) If the patient fails the Letters Attention Test Correct Answer: a) If the patient is negative for acute onset of mental status changes or fluctuating course Explanation: If the patient is negative for acute onset of mental status changes or fluctuating course, the delirium assessment is stopped, as this excludes delirium. 13. The "Letters Attention Test" is used to evaluate which feature of delirium? a) Disorganized thinking b) Inattention c) Acute onset of mental status changes d) Altered level of consciousness Correct Answer: b) Inattention Explanation: The Letters Attention Test is specifically designed to assess inattention, a key criterion in identifying delirium. More than two errors indicate positive inattention. 14. During the "Letters Attention Test," a patient squeezes the examiner's hand 4 times incorrectly. What does this finding suggest? a) The patient has disorganized thinking b) The patient has an altered level of consciousness c) The patient is negative for inattention d) The patient is positive for inattention Correct Answer: d) The patient is positive for inattention Explanation: If a patient makes more than two errors during the Letters Attention Test, they are considered positive for inattention, which is a key criterion for delirium. 15. Which of the following features must accompany inattention for a patient to be diagnosed with delirium? a) Elevated Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score b) Either disorganized thinking or altered level of consciousness c) Acute pain d) Persistent agitation Correct Answer: b) Either disorganized thinking or altered level of consciousness Explanation: After inattention is confirmed, the patient must exhibit either disorganized thinking or altered level of consciousness to meet the criteria for delirium. 16. A patient with a RASS score of +2 is being evaluated for delirium. What does this score indicate? a) Hyperactive behavior, suggesting altered level of consciousness b) Hypoactive delirium c) Normal mental status d) Calm and cooperative behavior Correct Answer: a) Hyperactive behavior, suggesting altered level of consciousness Explanation: A RASS score of +2 indicates hyperactive behavior, which suggests an altered level of consciousness—a key feature in identifying delirium. 17. Which tool is used to assess for disorganized thinking in a patient suspected of having delirium? a) Asking the patient to follow a specific command b) The Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) c) Asking the patient to recall a list of words d) The Letters Attention Test Correct Answer: a) Asking the patient to follow a specific command Explanation: Disorganized thinking is assessed by asking the patient a question or testing their ability to follow a specific command. 18. What is the next step in the assessment? a) Check for non-modifiable risk factors of delirium b) Assess for altered level of consciousness c) Confirm the patient is delirium-free d) Perform the Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU (CAM-ICU) Correct Answer: b) Assess for altered level of consciousness Explanation: After confirming fluctuating mental status and inattention, the next step is to check for altered level of consciousness if disorganized thinking is absent. 19. A patient responds appropriately to commands and has a RASS score of 0. What does this suggest? a) The patient does not exhibit delirium features b) The patient has an altered level of consciousness c) The patient has disorganized thinking d) The patient is experiencing inattention Correct Answer: a) The patient does not exhibit delirium features Explanation: A RASS score of 0 indicates the patient is calm and cooperative, suggesting no altered level of consciousness, which excludes delirium if other features are absent. 20. Which finding alone is sufficient to rule out delirium during the assessment process? a) Negative for acute onset of mental status changes or fluctuating course b) Negative for altered level of consciousness c) Negative on the Letters Attention Test d) Negative for disorganized thinking Correct Answer: a) Negative for acute onset of mental status changes or fluctuating course Explanation: If the patient does not have acute onset of mental status changes or fluctuating course, the assessment stops immediately, ruling out delirium. 21. Why is hypoactive delirium particularly concerning in critically ill patients? a) It is more aggressive and causes severe agitation. b) It is often undiagnosed and adversely affects patient outcomes. c) It has clear pharmacological treatment options. d) It is less common but easier to detect than hyperactive delirium. Non-pharmacological strategies are preferred. 24.
