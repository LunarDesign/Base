What is the main goal of Traction in Fracture Management?
Traction in fracture management aims primarily to align and fixate one or more bone fragments, maintain
good positioning, relax muscle spasms, and relieve pain. It involves a system of weights and pulleys that
apply a steady force on the injured limb. Because it pulls the bones together, traction ensures they heal in
correct alignment, and there are no complications, such as deformity.
What are the clinical features of Rhabdomyolysis?
Rhabdomyolysis's clinical features include muscle aches and pains, tenderness, and swelling. The
affected muscle groups may become weak. Patients also had dark urine, the result of myoglobin from
injured muscles being released into the blood and excreted as urine. Other symptoms include tiredness,
feeling sick to the stomach, and, in serious cases, drowsiness or confusion caused by electrolyte
imbalances.
How do we know when someone has rhabdomyolysis?
Rhabdomyolysis is diagnosed through a combination of clinical assessment and laboratory tests. Key
diagnostic markers for rhabdomyolysis include greatly elevated levels of creatine kinase (CK) in the
blood. As this enzyme is released from damaged muscle cells, it can cause compensatory dilation, leading
eventually (with prolonged ischemia) to frank necrosis formation. An elevated level thus usually confirms
the presence of myoglobin in urine. Besides this, additional tests may be required depending on the
findings of these procedures: serum electrolytes (especially bicarbonate), renal function studies, serum
calcium, uric acid, and iron-binding capacity.
